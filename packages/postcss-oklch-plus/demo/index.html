oklch, but better — perceptual model comparison

The same hue×lightness field, five ways. oklch is a naive 2-stop linear-gradient(in oklch …) over the full lightness range — literally what your browser does, over-clamping the endpoints into garbage at high chroma. safe(…) is that same gradient run through our resampler — 20 stops each clamped to the per-hue P3 boundary, emitted as plain CSS — so the browser renders it cleanly (the “teardrop”: chroma fades to gray at the poles, where no colour fits). The dark line is the true per-hue P3 boundary where the raw browser clips; on the lower bands the white line is where we clamp after H–K shifts lightness. ours and Nayatani‑1997 add the Helmholtz–Kohlrausch correction (our 3-harmonic fit vs. the real predictor via culori). Δ×20 is ours minus the reference, amplified.

Scan a row of constant lightness across hue. In oklch (your real browser) apparent brightness lurches, and beyond the contour the native render is clipping out of P3 — flattening and hue-shifting. (This is a deliberately naive 2-stop gradient over the full lightness range — the bug at its starkest; a hand-authored multi-stop gradient would fare better.) ours/Nayatani-1997 even the brightness out and clamp chroma along that same boundary. The Δ band is their difference ×20: it proves the fit is indistinguishably close, not bit-identical — the faint glow near yellow-green is the fit's largest residual (~0.5%), honestly visible at this amplification. On a non-P3 display the browser clips narrower than the P3 contour shown. Push chroma up to grow the effects.
